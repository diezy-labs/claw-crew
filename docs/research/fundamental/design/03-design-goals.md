## 3. Design Goals

## 3.1 Product goals

1. Make Quartermaster the natural starting point for every user request.
2. Make Fleet status and decisions understandable without reading raw logs.
3. Make a Quest feel like a real SOP/workflow, not an invisible background agent run.
4. Make Ships feel like persistent specialist teams, not containers named after projects.
5. Make Artifacts the center of value, evidence, review, and learning.
6. Preserve deep technical capability for developers, engineers, and operators.
7. Make BYOK/BYOM cost visible through Treasury without creating a platform-credit experience.
8. Keep pirate/maritime narrative memorable without making the UI childish or obscure.
9. Enable a Community user to understand the product with one active Ship and up to five persistent Crew Members.
10. Support future multi-Ship/Fleet UX without forcing that complexity on first use.

## 3.2 Experience goals

| Goal | Design outcome |
|---|---|
| Fast first value | First useful Artifact in under 20 minutes |
| Calm authority | Product feels capable and controlled, not noisy or playful for its own sake |
| Explainable autonomy | User can see why work was routed, what is running, and what needs a decision |
| Progressive depth | Simple entry for normal work; advanced controls are one clear step away |
| Trust | Cost, permissions, memory, approvals, and external actions are explicit |
| Delight | Narrative language, Quest progress, Artifact/Treasure flow, and visual identity make the product emotionally memorable |

## 3.3 Non-goals

```text
- Recreate a full visual workflow builder as the first interaction.
- Make pirate lore the only language in critical security/cost screens.
- Copy Kiro, Claude, or ChatGPT visual assets, wording, or UI layouts literally.
- Expose all current technical pages in primary navigation.
- Turn the product into a dashboard with 20 charts.
- Hide advanced technical capability permanently.
- Gamify spending, failures, or risky autonomous actions.
```
